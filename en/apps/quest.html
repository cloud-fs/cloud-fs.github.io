Clouds

All of CloudDrive2, running in the headset

Every account in one app

115, Aliyun Drive, Baidu Pan, Google Drive, OneDrive, Amazon S3, WebDAV (Nextcloud, Seafile and the rest), SFTP, FTP, SMB/CIFS and local network storage, all connected in one place. Browsing, search, uploads, downloads, cross-cloud copying, backup jobs and the WebDAV server are the same ones you use on the desktop.

The file browser filters by type — images, video, audio, documents, archives — so finding tonight's film in a drive holding a few hundred thousand files takes a moment, not a scroll.

The headset build has no local filesystem mounting: on Quest you cannot mount a cloud as a system drive. Everything else matches the desktop.

  • WebDAV server

Media

Cloud files, played where they are

The player reads from the cloud, so a 60 GB disc image never has to land on the headset's storage. Decoding, menus, audio tracks and subtitles all come with it.

  • Decoding and resolution

    Hardware-accelerated H.264 and H.265/HEVC up to 8K. AV1, MPEG-2, VC-1 and H.264 High Profile decode in software through FFmpeg. Containers include MP4, MKV, AVI, WebM, TS, M2TS, ISO, HLS and DASH.

  • HDR and Dolby Vision

    HDR10, HDR10+ and HLG, tone-mapped with ITU-R BT.2446-A. Dolby Vision covers profiles 5, 7, 8, 8.1 and 9.

  • Blu-ray and DVD ISOs

    Blu-ray ISOs play without extraction, with both HDMV and BD-J (Java) disc menus, multi-clip playlists, chapter navigation and PGS bitmap subtitles. DVD ISOs read IFO metadata and seek across chapters in multi-episode discs. Audio tracks switch between TrueHD, DTS-HD, FLAC and LPCM, with lossless badges.

  • Subtitles

    SRT, ASS/SSA with full libass styling, VTT, PGS bitmap subtitles and TX3G. External subtitle files are picked up automatically by filename.

Dolby Vision needs a Quest 3, Quest 3S or Quest Pro. Quest 2 hardware does not support Dolby Vision. A Profile 7 disc carries two picture layers and both are read, but dual-layer output needs a device that supports it; in the headset the picture arrives as single-layer Dolby Vision.

Projection modes

Flat cinema, 3D side-by-side and over-under, 360 monoscopic and stereoscopic, VR180 in half-SBS and half-OU, fisheye mono and stereo with the field of view read automatically from the file, equi-angular cubemap (the YouTube VR format), plain cubemap, and passthrough VR with alpha blending, packed alpha (DeoVR) or chroma key.

When you open a file the projection is taken from the video metadata, and falls back to a filename heuristic when there is none. If it guesses wrong, change it from the player.

In VR video, subtitles take on the depth of the picture automatically, so your eyes never have to refocus between the text and the scene. Flat video has no depth to follow, so its subtitles stay on the screen plane.

  • 360 mono / stereo
  • VR180 half-SBS / half-OU
  • Fisheye auto FOV
  • EAC · cubemap
  • Passthrough VR

Audiophile audio, up to DSD1024

FLAC, ALAC, APE, DSD (DSF and DFF), SACD ISO, WAV, AIFF, MP3, AAC, OGG and more stream straight from the cloud. A USB DAC gets bit-perfect output in either DSD over PCM (DoP) or Native DSD mode, and DSD64 through DSD1024 play natively.

DSD64 through DSD1024 are all supported. How far DoP and Native DSD actually go is set by the DAC you connect — the app outputs to what the DAC itself reports, and anything beyond that is converted to PCM.

Gapless playback, ReplayGain and CUE sheets are all supported. In cinema mode, 5.1 and 7.1 content becomes head-tracked spatial audio, so the soundstage stays with the screen when you turn your head.

  • DSD64 → DSD1024
  • DoP · Native DSD
  • SACD ISO
  • CUE sheets
  • Head-tracked spatial audio

AI subtitle translation

Generate translated subtitles for any video. CloudDrive VR translates the existing subtitle track first — including PGS bitmap subtitles read by OCR — and falls back to speech recognition only when there is none. Generated subtitles are saved next to the video, so the work you do once in the headset is there on your phone and your Mac too.

On Quest, bring your own translation API for the best results. There are no platform translation models in the headset to fall back on.

  • Bring your own API
  • PGS OCR
  • Saved for reuse

A multi-gigabyte PDF, open in seconds

The streaming PDF reader never pulls the whole book down. It fetches only the byte range the pages on screen actually need, so a multi-gigabyte art book typically goes from open to first page in seconds — no waiting for a download, and no complete copy left on the headset.

The reader is a panel like any other, at whatever size and distance suits you. Turning pages, zooming and jumping through the outline all fetch on demand, with page thumbnails, bookmarks and reading-position memory.

  • Fetches pages on demand
  • No full download
  • Bookmarks and position

More

The rest of what you'll use daily

  • Emby and Jellyfin client

    Connect your own Emby or Jellyfin servers and browse libraries with posters, cast and Continue Watching, all inside the headset. When your CloudDrive can reach the same file, Direct Stream plays it straight from the cloud at full quality — including formats servers can't transcode — while watch history stays in sync.

  • Or launched from another app

    CloudDrive VR also registers through the Android intent API, so Jellyfin, Emby and other media servers can hand playback straight to it. Resume position syncs back to the server, and remote Blu-ray ISO streams are detected automatically.

  • Controllers and hands

    Drive the interface with controllers or hand tracking. Thumbstick seeking accelerates exponentially and shows a thumbnail preview, so finding a scene takes one pass.

  • Transfers and backup

    Copy or move files between providers, with transfers you can pause and resume. Automated backup tasks run on a schedule, and whole folders upload recursively — the headset keeps them going while you watch something else.

  • Security

    Two-factor authentication, encrypted folders and active session monitoring, with API tokens that grant scoped, expiring access.

  • A WebDAV server of its own

    Your Quest headset is itself a WebDAV server: every cloud you have connected is served over a standard protocol, so third-party apps on the same network — players, file managers, backup tools — read your cloud content directly without signing in to each provider again.

  • 2D window and immersive mode

    Ordinary videos play in a 2D window; VR material switches to immersive mode with one click, and back again just as fast.

About the Pro features

Cloud management, file browsing, uploads, downloads and ordinary video, audio and image playback need no extra purchase. HDR and Dolby Vision, native DSD output (DoP and Native DSD), Blu-ray and DVD ISO playback, AI subtitle translation, the Emby and Jellyfin media client, and the VR video modes — 360, VR180, fisheye, EAC, passthrough and 3D SBS/OU — are Pro features, and free users get a 10-second demo of each.

They unlock exactly as they do everywhere else: with the one-time App Pro in-app purchase. App Pro is tied to your CloudDrive account rather than to a device or a store — buy it on an iPhone, a Mac or an Android phone, then sign in on the headset with the same account and it is already unlocked.
